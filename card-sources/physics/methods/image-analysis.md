# Sources of T-IMAGE-ANALYSIS

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | doi:10.1083/jcb.200903097 | full text | "Non-uniform illumination": uneven illumination makes equal concentrations read differently; divide by an image of a uniform sample, collected again each session. "Background fluorescence reduces dynamic range": subtract background measured next to the object. "The detector": saturated pixels cannot be used and cropping them out biases selection. "Resolution in the digital image": pixel size well below the optical resolution so small objects span several pixels. "Image processing and storage": bit-depth conversion, pseudo-colour and lossy compression alter intensities, and not all deconvolution suits quantitation |
| 2 | doi:10.1083/jcb.201812109 | full text | "Nonuniform illumination" and Fig. 4: flat-field division, an inaccurate flat-field adds error; camera offset measured without light and subtracted. Table 2: magnification and pixel size checked with a stage micrometer. "Experimenter bias" and "Strategies to reduce bias": fields chosen because they look good bias results; automated or predetermined field selection and blinding; criteria for skipping fields set in advance. "Measurement validation": validate analysis on known data and do not tune parameters on the data to be analysed. "Methods reporting": software versions and settings reported |
| 3 | doi:10.1091/mbc.E17-05-0276 | full text | Introduction and "Bias in experimental design": choice of region of interest affects reproducibility; random or predetermined positions, tiling the whole well, coded samples. "Rigor in image acquisition" and Fig. 2: Nyquist sampling at least twice the resolution, undersampling aliases; avoid saturation; raising bit depth after acquisition does not improve images. "Common obstacles during image processing": changing display curves or saving as JPEG alters raw values; analyse the raw file |
| 4 | doi:10.1038/nmeth.4397 | full text | Step 1, "Field-of-view illumination correction": uneven illumination corrupts segmentation and intensities; prospective references often incomplete; compute corrections per batch. Step 1, "Segmentation": parameters tuned by visual inspection, labelled ground truth for assessment. Step 2, "Image quality control": blur and saturated-pixel measures, thresholds set from their distributions. Step 3: batch effects from technical variation can lead to false conclusions |
| 5 | doi:10.1038/ncomms14836 | full text | Introduction: image model with multiplicative flat-field and additive dark-field; shading compromises quantitative analysis and mosaics. Results: correction changed single-cell quantification and removed stitching errors. Discussion: prospective references can mismatch the sample; background drifts over time |
| 6 | doi:10.6028/jres.098.033 | full text | Sec. 3 and Conclusion: magnification errors across many instruments, with large spread; jumps between magnification ranges; x and y calibrations often not equal; each instrument to be calibrated and rechecked regularly against a reference standard |
| 7 | doi:10.1016/j.ultramic.2015.12.002 | full text | Sec. 2.1: drift affects the slow scan direction much more strongly than the fast one. Sec. 2.2: linear drift is an affine distortion (shear, stretch); jumps and hysteresis displace scan lines. Sec. 3.3: streaks are especially pronounced in strain maps because differentiation magnifies noise. Method: orthogonal scan pairs used to correct the distortion |
| 8 | https://gwyddion.net/documentation/user-guide-en/ | full text | "Data levelling and background subtraction": higher-degree levelling can suppress real features and alter statistics; arc, sphere and median backgrounds remove features larger than their radius or kernel. "Scan line artefacts": row alignment modifies data heavily and should be applied with care, using masks. "Grain analysis": threshold, Otsu and watershed marking; removing grains that touch the image edge. "Tip convolution artefacts": tip shape distorts images; certainty map marks where the tip touched at one point only |
| 9 | doi:10.1038/s41592-021-01284-3 | full text | Introduction: models perform well mainly on images like their training data and can produce artefacts that are hard to identify. "Assessing DL model predictions" and Box 1: validate against ground truth with error maps; segmentation metrics such as IoU and F1. "Concluding remarks": image restoration is prone to undetected artefacts because it adds information from training data; absolute intensities after denoising may not be quantifiable. "Reporting the use of DL in publications": tool and version, training data, hyperparameters, validation metric, score and evaluation images kept out of training |
| 10 | doi:10.1016/j.ultramic.2019.112847 | abstract | Abstract: simulations show that conventional boundary methods (active contour and binarisation at half maximum) give size-dependent errors in nanoparticle diameter from scanning electron microscope images; a boundary set from image formation reduced the uncertainty |
