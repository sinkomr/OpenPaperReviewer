II: total energy output not shown to exceed total input over a run; most reported excesses are power, not energy, measurements and are smaller than the 1.527 V x I enthalpy term; calibration non-linearity, time dependence and data-acquisition accuracy listed as problems. App. 2.C: the integral heat transfer coefficient at the operating temperature is needed and must be re-measured as conditions such as electrolyte level change; thermocouple offsets from corrosion and electrical pickup; usual measurements blind to the ac component of input power; recombination and current efficiency below 100% appear as excess heat. Sec. III: a watt of D+D fusion implies of order 10^11 to 10^12 reactions per second, readily observable; neutron and proton branches equal to within 10%; cosmic-ray neutron rate can vary by 20% or more with pressure and solar activity; some counters sensitive to humidity or vibration; electrolysis enriches the tritium already present in heavy water; one helium-4 report traced to atmospheric contamination; helium limits compared with the amount expected from the claimed heat. App. 3.B: tritium production requires a complete inventory at start and end, with electrolytic enrichment taken into account. Sec. IV: cathodes pick up deposits from the anode, glass container and electrolyte; loading ratio generally not determined in gas-loading work, and weight-gain values reliable only if losses are minimized and deposits corrected for; compositions and microstructures of electrodes seldom characterised |
