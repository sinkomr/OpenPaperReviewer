# Sources of T-BLIND-ANALYSIS

| 1 | doi:10.1146/annurev.nucl.55.090704.151521 | full text | Sec. 2: biased cut choice, cuts tuned to include or exclude individual events, and stopping once the result matches expectation (called probably the most common bias); unblinding decided through internal review; selections added after unblinding to be stated explicitly; blinding does not ensure correctness, does not remove instrumental biases, and cannot cover event-by-event human inspection. Sec. 3.1: hidden box needs known signal location and a background estimate independent of events in the box, should be larger than the final region, and ambiguous outcomes should be planned for. Secs. 3.2.2 and 3.2.3: offset drawn from a wide Gaussian covering earlier results, hidden sign, separate seeds for parallel analyses, sign lifted early for systematics; distributions that reveal an asymmetry hidden and fit curves not overlaid, residuals used instead. Sec. 3.3: added events must mimic signal; adding alone leaks information, so an unknown fraction is also removed, in blocks for time-correlated data. Sec. 3.4: prescaled samples chosen randomly and spanning time variations, discarded if large; hidden box unusable when the mass is unknown |
| 2 | https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0312102 | full text | Sec. 2: bias from cut choice can be of the order of the statistical uncertainty, agreement with earlier results says nothing about the correctness of a measurement, and this bias cannot be estimated numerically. Sec. 3.3: the hidden variable for decay-time plots, with the fitted curve not overlaid and residuals used. Sec. 3.4: branching fractions and searches for bumps at unknown location are hard to blind. Sec. 4: review and changes made while still blind; unblinding once a wider group or review committee is satisfied |
| 3 | doi:10.1093/mnras/staa965 | full text | Secs. 1 and 2.3: shifting only the final parameters is a thin cover with a high risk of accidental unblinding and can be compromised by plotting a model over the data, so blinding earlier in the pipeline is preferred. Sec. 2.1: the transformation must be able to move the result into or out of the region the analysts expect. Sec. 2.2: it must not change the outcome of validation tests or the maximum likelihood. Sec. 3.1: unblind only after the validation tests pass. Sec. 4: blinding tested on simulated data by the change in goodness of fit; unblinding criteria such as a fit-quality threshold |
| 4 | doi:10.1162/99608f92.250f995b | full text | Sec. 6.6: selection tuned on the data gives p values too small and signals too large; the data selection, classification and statistical procedures are fixed before unblinding and the result accepted without change, or the analysis is not fully blind; control samples examined first can reveal unexpected backgrounds or miscalibrations; early data used to tune the analysis must be left out of the final result for it to be called fully blind |
