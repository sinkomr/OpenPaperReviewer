| 3 | doi:10.1038/s41598-022-23341-3 | full text | Abstract: storage changed angles by up to 60%, and evaporation can lower angles by 30 to 50% within 10 minutes in dry air. Introduction: a deposited-drop angle may be a metastable result, and advancing and receding angles are measured dynamically. Results: storage effects traced to differing adsorbed carbon; ultrasonic cleaning lowered angles; tap water gave lower angles than purified water on hydrophilic samples; larger drops gave lower angles through gravity and very small drops are disturbed by the needle; pre-exposure to saturated humidity lowered hydrophilic angles. Conclusions: control preparation, storage and cleaning to give defined sample states, and measure quickly after deposition unless evaporation is suppressed |

| 5 | doi:10.1038/s41598-017-00607-9 | full text | Introduction: angles above 150° are typically undervalued and depend on the optical setup and image analysis, and many studies do not say whether an angle is advancing or receding. Results: without enough magnification a short-capillary-length liquid was underestimated by about 10°; advancing and receding angles reported separately. Conclusions: lens magnification chosen inversely proportional to the liquid's capillary length |
