| 1 | doi:10.1002/anie.201908154 | full text | Read as PMC7154527. Enhancement-factor discussion: the substrate EF compares signal per molecule with normal Raman per molecule; the reference needs the same mode and instrument conditions; molecule numbers need coverage, orientation and active area, hard for colloids; relative EFs against standards. Hot-spot discussion: a small fraction of the surface gives most of the signal, so imaging alone predicts performance poorly and correlative microscopy is advised; batch-to-batch and within-surface reproducibility distinguished. Analyte discussion: strongly binding, non-resonant reporters recommended as standards; resonant dyes have cross-sections that can change on adsorption |
| 2 | doi:10.1021/jp0687908 | abstract | Abstract: gives rigorous definitions of SERS enhancement factors for analytical and single-molecule use, which need careful characterisation of the probe's non-SERS cross-section; single-molecule EFs determined by bi-analyte SERS and vibrational pumping; EFs near 10^7 suffice for single-molecule detection, far below the often-quoted 10^14 |
| 3 | doi:10.1126/science.1159499 | abstract | Abstract: site enhancements on a silver-coated nanosphere substrate span about 2.8 x 10^4 to 4.1 x 10^10; the hottest sites, 63 in a million, give about a quarter of the total signal |
| 4 | doi:10.1021/jp054732v | abstract | Abstract: using two analytes at once gives clear evidence of single- or few-molecule signals and avoids the uncertainties of relying on very low concentrations |
| 5 | doi:10.1021/acsnano.9b04224 | full text | Read as PMC6990571 (first part of the text). "Surface-Enhanced Raman Scattering Mechanisms: Electromagnetic Field Enhancement": the fourth-power field estimate is good to about an order of magnitude; below about 1 nm, nonlocal effects and tunnelling change the response and usually reduce enhancement. "Surface-Enhanced Raman Scattering Mechanisms: Chemical" and "Modified Electronic Absorbance of Molecules on Surfaces": charge-transfer enhancement, and an absorption shift on adsorption giving an apparent increase in EF. "Modeling of Molecular Species at the Surface": a deuterated isotopologue used to decide whether spectra come from single molecules; light-generated electrons convert adsorbed molecules to new species |
| 6 | doi:10.1038/ncomms11495 | full text | Read as PMC4895716. Introduction and sections on nonlocal and tunnelling effects: local classical models predict field enhancement growing without limit as the gap shrinks; nonlocal screening reduces it in narrow gaps and tunnelling quenches it below a subnanometre threshold; gap sizes are hard to measure and results depend on gap morphology; SERS enhancement measured on dimers is quenched below the tunnelling threshold |
| 7 | doi:10.1021/ph5004237 | full text | Read as PMC4416469 (Europe PMC full text). Deposition rules: base pressure, rate and temperature change grain size, roughness and optical losses; films made with optimised recipes have much lower imaginary permittivity than tabulated reference data, which should not be used to model ideal structures; optical constants measured by spectroscopic ellipsometry on smooth films |
| 8 | doi:10.1038/s41377-020-00345-0 | full text | Read as PMC7321931. Single-particle temperature formulas severely underestimate sample heating because of collective heating; thermocouples away from the illuminated area underestimate the rise; over a narrow power range an Arrhenius dependence looks linear, so a wide range is needed; procedures that vary beam diameter, compare wavelengths at matched absorbance, use time scales, and measure temperature by infrared imaging or thermometry |
| 9 | doi:10.1039/c4cs00131a | full text | Read as author manuscript PMC4641313. Sec. 3: dark-field spectra corrected by background subtraction and division by a white-light standard, and for substrate scattering. Sec. 5 (photothermal and absorption methods): photothermal imaging gives absorption for particles too small to scatter. Sec. 6: a diffraction-limited spot cannot be assumed to be one particle; correlated electron microscopy needed; electron beams alter spectra, so optics first. Sec. 8: ensemble spectra are inhomogeneously broadened; single-particle linewidths give the intrinsic damping |
| 10 | doi:10.1063/1.363956 | abstract | Abstract: with gap-width feedback such as shear force, near-field images can represent the probe's path rather than the optical properties of the sample; many reported results may have been affected or dominated by this artefact |
| 11 | doi:10.1515/nanoph-2021-0565 | full text | Read as PMC11501567. Introduction: additive background suppressed by higher-harmonic demodulation, multiplicative background by interferometric detection. Sec. 2.1: normalising to a flat reference such as Au or Si cancels far-field terms only when sample and reference reflect similarly. Sec. 2.2: far-field reflections near the tip change near-field amplitude and phase across a uniform patch, with interference near edges; ratios of harmonic orders remove them |
