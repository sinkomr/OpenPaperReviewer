| 1 | doi:10.1002/pip.3919 | full text | Sec. 1 (Introduction): the main criterion for inclusion is independent measurement by a recognised test centre; three eligible area definitions (total, aperture, designated illumination area); masks must have a simple single aperture, masks with multiple openings are ineligible, and "active area" efficiencies are excluded; minimum areas of 1 cm² for a one-sun cell and 0.05 cm² for a one-sun notable exception; EQE and current-voltage curves are included with tabled results |
| 2 | doi:10.1038/nmat4473 | full text | Whole editorial: the checklist asks for scan conditions, cell size and number of samples, and whether performance was confirmed by an independent certification laboratory; several groups favour mandatory certification for record claims; without certification, extra care in describing the procedure and statistical evidence of reproducibility, with average values reported alongside the best device; hysteresis may need a tuned protocol |
| 3 | doi:10.1002/aenm.202100728 | full text | Sec. 2 opening: standard test conditions include a device temperature of 25 °C. Secs. 1 and 2.1: poorly defined area is among the most common errors; light piping through unmasked glass overestimates short-circuit current; area measured with a calibrated microscope and repeated to estimate uncertainty. Sec. 2.2: spectral mismatch factor from simulator spectrum and spectral responses of reference cell and device, kept within 0.97 to 1.03; EQE without light bias distorts the spectrum and inflates current by about 1.5%; integrated EQE current within about 10% of the J-V value, or measured current below 98% of the normalised-EQE limit for glass-encapsulated cells, with failures usually traced to area or light piping; simulator spectral match, uniformity and stability. Secs. 2.3 and 4: accredited laboratories use steady-state protocols; fast-scan efficiencies of nearly 200 submitted cells deviated by about 20% relative and up to 40% from steady-state values; scans repeated after the steady-state measurement to check degradation |
| 4 | doi:10.1063/1.4960759 | full text | Abstract and opening section: hysteresis depends on scan rate, device and measurement history, so standard J-V measurements are not reproducible; scan direction and speed can change results by up to 50%; averaging scan directions and hysteresis indices are not representative; comparing short-circuit current with integrated EQE depends on light bias and chopping frequency; maximum power point tracking for at least 60 s as the first measurement. Concluding section: only stabilised results represent operating efficiency |
| 5 | doi:10.1038/s41566-019-0543-y | full text | "Quantum efficiencies": three collection geometries; detector not in contact with the device; a small-solid-angle detector with the Lambertian assumption misses wide-angle emission or overestimates EQE for forward-directed emission, so measure the angular profile; aperture masks against waveguided edge and scattered light; spectral calibration weighted by the emission spectrum; cross-check by two methods; distance errors; EQE across orders of magnitude of current density; extraction efficiency affected by photon recycling and dipole orientation; comparison with photoluminescence efficiency. "Device emission colour and intensity": EQE meaningful only at practical luminance, at least 100 cd m⁻² for displays. "Stability and device lifetime": sweeps at two rates in both directions, asynchronous readout errors, spectra at peak EQE and maximum brightness, with allowance for the change in photodetector response when the spectrum shifts. Table 1: reporting checklist |
| 6 | doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.85.1245 | full text | Abstract and Sec. I A 6: about 80% of generated photons are trapped in the layer structure; the light modes are set mainly by the thin-film structure and its refractive indices and absorption; the fraction reaching the far field is typically about 20%, and waveguide and surface plasmon modes cannot be extracted by external techniques. Sec. I B 1: EQE is correct only if the angular distribution is measured with an integrating sphere or goniometer, and the Lambertian assumption has been shown not to hold. Sec. II D: rule-of-thumb limit of about 20% EQE for flat devices without outcoupling improvement. Sec. IV: outcoupling structures |
