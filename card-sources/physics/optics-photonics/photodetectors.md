# Sources of T-PHOTODETECTORS

| # | Identifier | Access | Where it supports the item |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | doi:10.1038/s41566-025-01759-1 | full text | Consensus statement, read on the publisher page. "Responsivity and external quantum efficiency": monitor power with a calibrated reference detector, define illuminated areas, aperture over most of the active area with uniform irradiance; overfilling can overestimate EQE, underfilling misrepresents non-uniform devices; modulation frequency well below the device bandwidth. "Photoconductive gain versus EQE": EQE and gain are distinct, high apparent EQE with injecting contacts is not by itself better performance, gain-bandwidth product as benchmark, gain raises noise. "Noise": shot-noise-only models often inaccurate, theoretical noise to be labelled and not used for superlative claims, white-noise assumption for bandwidth conversion. "Noise-equivalent power and detectivity": responsivity measured near NEP, D* valid only if noise scales with the square root of area and bandwidth, which holds for shot-noise-limited vertical photodiodes without gain but is not universal, otherwise apparent or fixed-bandwidth values. "Speed of response": plateau conditions for rise times, flat low-frequency response for 3 dB bandwidth, first-order relations not assumed, several power levels. "Linearity and LDR": report the log-log slope, define limits by deviation from unit slope, not by dark current or noise floor. "Characterization, reporting and benchmarking in context": all metrics at a common voltage, dark current and responsivity rechecked around noise and time-domain tests |
| 2 | doi:10.1038/s41467-023-37635-1 | full text | Read as PMC10115793. "Device effective area": effective optical area sets incident power, Gaussian spot errors, area depends on device geometry and enhancement structures, scanning photocurrent mapping advised. "Responsivity": laser spot-size errors, calibration against a standard detector, IQE as EQE over absorption. Box 1: photoconductive, photovoltaic, photothermoelectric and bolometric mechanisms have distinct signatures. "Dark current and noise": g-r noise scales with gain, ignoring it overestimates D*; 1/f noise not to be dismissed without measurement. "Noise equivalent power and specific detectivity": frequency stated, responsivity and noise bandwidths matched, D* depends on correct area. "Time parameters": 10-90% rise from a full periodic signal at steady state, 3 dB definition, gain-bandwidth limit in photoconductors |
| 3 | doi:10.1038/s41467-018-07643-7 | full text | Read as PMC6288162 via Europe PMC. Discussion of figures of merit: quantum efficiency, responsivity and noise should be measured under identical conditions and reported against bandwidth, temperature and wavelength; shot-noise-limited noise is rarely the case, especially for photoconductors, 1/f noise often dominates, so D* should rest on measured noise. Discussion of gain: gain set by lifetime trades against speed, and the gain-bandwidth product rather than gain is the figure of merit |
| 4 | doi:10.1039/C5CS00106D | full text | Read as preprint arXiv:1506.00679 (Sections 1 to 4). Sec. 2.1.1: photoconductive gain as lifetime over transit time, photogating by trapped carriers giving large gain. Sec. 2.1.3: traps make photocurrent sublinear in power and give high gain at the cost of speed. Secs. 2.2.1 to 2.2.3: photothermoelectric and bolometric effects, how they differ in bias dependence, sign and spatial origin. Sec. 4.1: responsivity highest at low power, so gain comparisons depend on excitation level |
| 5 | doi:10.1063/5.0184847 | abstract | Abstract: linear dynamic range values are often misreported; the unity-slope criterion and the choice of definition for the deviation point change the result; criteria proposed for consistent reporting |
| 6 | doi:10.1088/0026-1394/35/4/48 | abstract | NIST publication record, abstract: spatial responsivity of photodiodes varies with wavelength, sometimes strongly near the band gap, mainly from material defects, and this can add significantly to measurement uncertainty |
