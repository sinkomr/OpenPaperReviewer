| 1 | doi:10.1038/nmeth.2448 | full text | Introduction: localisation precision alone or labelling density alone does not measure resolution, which also depends on labelling, structure and processing. Main text: FRC from two statistically independent half-sets with a threshold of about 1/7; repeated localisations of one emitter correlate the halves and give too optimistic values, corrected with an estimated parameter Q; trade-off between labelling density and precision. Discussion: the measure depends on signal-to-noise ratio and spectral content |
| 2 | doi:10.1038/s43586-021-00038-x | full text | Box 1: resolution limited by precision and by sampling density; a reanalysis of the early Nyquist criterion suggested about fivefold denser sampling; FRC needs a user-defined threshold and can hide spatial variation; nuclear pore complexes as a reference structure with known distances. Fluorescent labelling strategies: linkage error of about 10 nm for antibodies, about 20 nm with indirect labelling. Quantitative analyses: repeated blinking gives artefactual clusters and overcounting; overlapping emitters and drift as artefacts |
| 3 | doi:10.1038/nphoton.2015.279 | full text | Opening section: resolution in coherent imaging is defined and reported inconsistently, and noise affects resolution limits. "Standard part 1": report the expected non-zero spatial-frequency coverage of the system; reported distance metrics vary (half-pitch, edge-to-edge, width of a single feature, which varies with contrast). "Standard part 2": resolution beyond the physical limit by assuming a bounded sample, sparsity, positivity or a regularisation term; such a priori assumptions must be stated, with the samples to which the method applies. "Standard part 3": a spoke (Siemens star) target, with resolution reported at several positions across the field of view |
| 4 | doi:10.1038/srep15915 | full text | Opening section: linear SIM doubles conventional resolution laterally and axially. "Structured illumination data checks": side lobes and intensity dips around bright features indicate mismatch between the sample conditions and the optical transfer function, mostly from spherical aberration caused by depth and refractive index; sample motion, illumination variation between angles and bleaching checked in raw data; reconstructed noise where modulation contrast is low; sub-optimal reconstruction parameters diagnosed. Methods: modulation contrast-to-noise ratio, reconstructed Fourier plots and intensity histograms, with a suggested Wiener filter setting |
| 5 | doi:10.1038/nmeth.4605 | full text | Main text: reconstructions can lose, merge or brighten structures, with errors concentrated where emitters are dense; comparison with a diffraction-limited reference of the same field gives an error map and global scores; local FRC resolution does not track local error; artefacts below the reference resolution are not detected |
| 6 | doi:10.1038/s41592-021-01284-3 | full text | Figure 1 legend: a model performs well mainly on images similar to its training data. Figure 2 legend: when new data are not well represented in the training data, the model may generate artefacts and biases that are hard to identify. "Assessing DL model predictions": comparison with ground truth using error maps and similarity metrics; predictions should be closer to ground truth than the raw input; structures not easily visible in the raw data should be cross-validated. "Reporting the use of DL in publications": describe the training data, model version and main hyperparameters, and the validation, with evaluation data absent from training data |
| 7 | doi:10.1088/2515-7647/ac76f9 | full text | Introduction: ballistic photons decay exponentially with depth. Sec. 6: guide stars needed to focus inside scattering media, each with its own access requirements; in vivo decorrelation time around 1 ms. Sec. 10: decorrelation times of 50 ms to 2.5 s in living tissue; learning-based experimental results so far only for thin diffusers. Sec. 12: a network trained on a fixed diffuser is susceptible to changes of the scattering medium. Sec. 14: memory-effect angular range inversely proportional to sample thickness, limiting field of view; convergence instability of iterative phase retrieval; acquisition must be shorter than decorrelation time |
| 8 | doi:10.1038/s41592-019-0364-4 | full text | Results, "Realistic 3D simulations", and Methods 2.1: experimental data lack ground truth, so 36 software packages were assessed on realistic simulated data sets with known fluorophore positions at several densities and noise levels. Discussion: the choice of localisation software greatly affects the final image, and high-density 3D performance was mediocre at high and poor at low signal-to-noise ratio |
