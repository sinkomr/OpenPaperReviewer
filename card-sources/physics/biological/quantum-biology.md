| 1 | doi:10.1126/sciadv.aaz4888 | full text | "Collective excitations and energy migration": whether sunlight generates coherence is heavily debated. "Theoretical considerations": coherence is not uniquely quantum and electronic excitation can be described by classical models; interexciton coherences decay within about 100 fs at room temperature, shorter than intercomplex transfer. "Experimental considerations": assigning long-lived beats from frequency and sometimes phase alone is insufficient. "Studies of photosynthetic excitons": early long-lived beats observed at cryogenic temperatures; rephasing and non-rephasing oscillation maps compared with model vibrational and excitonic systems (Fig. 4). "Present status of experiments": mutants with different exciton splittings left beat frequencies unchanged; polarisation-controlled 2DES assigned long-lived beats mainly to ground-state vibrations; at physiological temperature no long-lived low-frequency electronic beats; working picture of incoherent downhill hopping |
