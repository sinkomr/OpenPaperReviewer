| 1 | doi:10.1038/s41592-018-0085-0 | full text | Results, correction factors and procedure: background, leakage from donor-only, direct excitation from acceptor-only, gamma by global, single-species or per-molecule schemes, beta for alternating excitation, applied in a stated order; without ALEX low-FRET values shift and acceptor bleaching is hard to see; correction moved a low-FRET peak from about 0.25 to 0.15; systematic variation of gamma gives an error of about 10%. Distance sections: apparent distance from mean efficiency is FRET-averaged, not a physical distance, and accessible-volume models give the mean-position distance; Förster radius uncertainty dominated by kappa squared and refractive index; distances below about 3 nm less reliable; dye mobility checked by anisotropy and lifetimes |
| 2 | doi:10.7554/eLife.60416 | full text | "Conjugation": compare labelled and unlabelled molecule function, report labelling efficiency, stochastic labelling with dyes of different properties causes problems. "Immobilization": non-specific binding, alternative schemes, cross-validation. "Spectroscopic characterization": blinking, bleaching, protein-induced enhancement, dye stacking altering kappa squared create artificial FRET species; single-labelled lifetime and anisotropy controls; ALEX or PIE. "Identification" and "User bias": burst search and selection criteria affect histograms; report the fraction of molecules analysed. "Detecting dynamics": averaging of transitions faster than the sampling time, width compared with the shot-noise limit, lifetime-versus-efficiency plots, BVA and 2CDE. "Slow dynamics": hidden Markov models and their remaining accuracy problems. "Determining absolute FRET efficiencies from fluorescence intensities": gamma as the major contribution to uncertainty. "Inter-dye distances", subsections "Fluorescence quantum yield" and "Dye transition dipole orientation factor": quantum yield depends on the labelling position and should be determined for each; kappa squared uncertainty bounded from time-resolved anisotropies; the FRET-averaged distance differs from the distance between mean dye positions. "Dye models": accessible-volume calculations |
| 3 | doi:10.1038/nmeth.1208 | full text | "Surface immobilization" and "The sample chamber": polyethylene glycol passivation for proteins; accept a surface when non-specific spots are under 10% of specifically tethered ones. "FRET efficiency": leakage and direct excitation from single-labelled molecules, gamma from acceptor photobleaching; kappa squared near two thirds only when anisotropy is below about 0.2; protein binding can alter gamma. "Enhancing photostability": oxygen scavenging lengthens dark states, triplet quenchers reduce blinking. "Interpreting data: pitfalls and tips": diagnose blinking with photostabilizers, direct acceptor excitation and intensity dependence; single-step photobleaching; features in under about 5% of molecules are hard to separate from artefacts; hidden Markov modelling for states and rates |
| 4 | doi:10.1073/pnas.0401690101 | full text | Introduction: inactive acceptors give species with donor-only characteristics, and substoichiometric labelling limits single-laser FRET. "Principle of ALEX" and "Sorting Species with Different D–A Stoichiometry and D–A Distance Using FAMS": the stoichiometry ratio separates donor-only, acceptor-only and doubly labelled species, so doubly labelled molecules with efficiency near zero are still identified. "FAMS Can Sense Fluorophore Brightness": stoichiometry responds to changes in dye brightness such as donor quenching |
| 5 | doi:10.1039/c3cs60201j | full text | "History and chemical basis of PIFE": cyanine isomerization competes with fluorescence and a bound protein restricts it, raising the quantum yield; a protein near one dye can raise its intensity without the anticorrelated change of the other. "Application – determination of the protein binding constant, substrate specificity, binding position and kinetics": enhancement depends on the distance of the bound protein from the dye; confirm that the dye does not perturb activity by comparison with standard assays |
| 6 | doi:10.1016/j.bpj.2011.01.066 | full text | Introduction and "BVA can distinguish between static and dynamic heterogeneity": broadening beyond shot noise shows heterogeneity but not its origin, and a static three-species mixture and a two-state dynamic model fit the same histogram. "Burst variance analysis": compares the standard deviation within bursts with the shot-noise expectation; changes in quantum yield or orientation factor can mimic dynamics and need controls |
| 7 | doi:10.1038/s41467-022-33023-3 | full text | "The archetypal 2-state system": tools agree for separated states, most rates slightly underestimated; some reported uncertainties below the finite-data limit. "Directional sequences in a non-equilibrium steady-state system": averaging within a frame creates apparent intermediate efficiencies. "States with overlapping FRET efficiencies": states sharing an efficiency are inferred only kinetically, from multi-exponential dwell-time distributions, and tools without kinetic heterogeneity deviated most. "Full complexity of a black-box experiment": model selection and state allocation the main challenges, the number of states varied between tools, high intensity variation between molecules. Discussion, Table 2: simulate the inferred model and reanalyse, report connectivity, give 95% confidence intervals |
