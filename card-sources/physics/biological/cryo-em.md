# Sources of T-CRYO-EM

| 1 | doi:10.1016/j.str.2011.12.014 | full text | Map resolution section and recommendation: thresholds not uniformly reported; full FSC curve to Nyquist for masked and unmasked maps and a statement of whether the half-datasets were separated from the start; an unmasked map from the last refinement cycle to be produced and deposited; visible features should match the claimed resolution, such as helical pitch and strand separation better than 4.5 Å; map manipulations such as thresholding and masking to be reported. Statistical assessment of the map: bias from a starting model and overfitting to be estimated; local FSC measures aid interpretation. Model criteria: map-model correlation, assessment as independent as possible of the fitting target |
| 2 | doi:10.1016/j.ultramic.2013.06.004 | full text | Introduction and Results: high-resolution noise can be reinforced in the map and give a false resolution; the two avoidance methods are fully independent half-sets and orientation search at limited resolution. Method: Fourier components beyond a chosen resolution replaced by noise (phase randomisation) and the corrected FSC computed from the true and noise-substituted curves. Results, masking: a tight mask with a sharp boundary gives false FSC at high resolution, while a gradual fall-off is tolerated by the correction. Introduction and Discussion: 0.143 criterion for maps from half the data, 0.5 suggested by others when overfitting is present, and no consensus on an acceptable amount of overfitting |
| 3 | doi:10.1073/pnas.1314449110 | full text | Abstract: data that are largely noise, aligned to a template, give maps resembling the template. Opening paragraphs and Problems: small particles carry too little information in each image to determine orientation. Validation tools: half-sets split at the start, even a shared mask can create false correlation, noise substitution, tilt pairs. New pitfall: radial density streaks often come from overfitted noise. Future precautions: confirm particles are visible, pick by hand in any initial analysis, do not classify particles picked by cross-correlation |
| 4 | doi:10.1074/jbc.REV118.005602 | full text | Sample preparation: air-water interface and support films cause preferred orientation and resolution anisotropy. Computational image analysis: accuracy of 3D classification for distinct and sub-stoichiometric populations varies; number of classes and significance of differences are open questions. Map and model validation: half-map FSC at typically 0.143, the shape of the FSC curve signals overfitting or heterogeneity; 3D FSC and angular distribution plots; local resolution recommended as standard; no standard for sharpening and thresholding; map-to-model FSC against a free half-map; model metrics not used in refinement |
| 5 | doi:10.1038/nmeth.4347 | full text | Main text: preferred orientation under-samples Fourier space, stretching the map and adding artefactual density, and can give an apparently high-resolution map that is largely incorrect; global FSC hides this. 3D FSC over angular cones with a sphericity measure; Euler angle distributions to be reported; map-to-model FSC as an external check; tilted collection as a remedy |
| 6 | doi:10.1038/nmeth.2727 | full text | Opening paragraphs: the FSC gives one resolution for the whole map and its threshold choice is debated. Results: local resolution varies within single-particle maps and subtomogram averages, lower at edges and in peripheral domains; local estimates mostly lay between the 0.143 and 0.5 FSC values. Methods: statistical test at each voxel, reported with its significance level |
| 7 | doi:10.1038/s41592-020-01051-w | full text | Results, overall and local quality: two-thirds of models had peptide geometry errors; misoriented peptides, sequence misalignment and missing ligands were common. Evaluating metrics: fit-to-map measures differ in dependence on resolution, noise and threshold; Ramachandran statistics lose value as validation when used as restraints. Discussion, recommendations: use several measures, including map-model FSC compared with the half-map FSC, Q-score, EMRinger, CaBLAM and per-residue local scores |
| 8 | doi:10.1042/BCJ20200715 | full text | Introduction and Subtomogram averaging: tilt limited to about ±60° gives the missing wedge and anisotropic resolution, compensated by wedge masks or 3D sampling functions and averaging many orientations; half-sets split before alignment, with FSC between them. Particle picking: templates from existing structures risk reference bias and should be low-pass filtered; the missing wedge can hide particles. Tilt-series alignment and CTF correction sections: alignment accuracy and defocus gradient. Discussion: in situ classification of small or rare particles remains hard |
