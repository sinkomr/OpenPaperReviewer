| 1 | doi:10.1038/nphoton.2011.35 | full text | Introduction: SQL and Heisenberg scalings defined relative to the number of probes, photons and repetitions used. IX.C.1: the GHZ advantage is usually cancelled by the faster decoherence of the entangled state; NOON states are fragile |
| 3 | doi:10.1116/5.0007577 | full text | Sec. Sec. III.D.1: higher NOON states rely on post-selection. Sec. IV (opening): outputs periodic in the phase leave it ambiguous without a changing control phase or adaptive probes; ultimate bounds are guaranteed only asymptotically, not for a limited number of probes |

Sec. I.C: the measurement that saturates the quantum Cramér-Rao bound depends on the parameter already being known; Bayesian and adaptive schemes address prior knowledge. Sec. II.A: a single probe used sequentially N times reaches the same Heisenberg scaling as an entangled state of N probes |

Sec. 5.4: the SQL of an interferometer is the lowest total of uncorrelated shot noise and radiation-pressure noise, and squeezing either quadrature alone does not go below it.
