| 1 | doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.83.1589 | full text | Sec. II.B (Tc vs doping): resistive onset and zero-resistance Tc plotted as separate points; about 10% shielding, against nearly full shielding in bulk samples, argues for a small volume fraction such as a sheath or filaments; field-cooled expulsion only a few per cent because of pinning; missing heat-capacity jump attributed to a small superconducting fraction. Sec. II.D (Tc vs pressure): shear strain in solidified media can make results non-reproducible; non-hydrostatic stress stabilised islands of another phase and filamentary superconductivity. Sec. III.A (Resistivity and Susceptibility): resistance reaches zero in some samples with no bulk signs of superconductivity; trace magnetic impurity phases nearly universal; one compound's superconductivity sample dependent. Sec. III.B (Specific Heat): heat capacity is a bulk measurement, unlike resistivity, which can be dominated by one-dimensional pathways; large residual electronic term as a sample-quality issue; equal-entropy construction for broad transitions; jump ratio differing between samples. Sec. V (opening) and V.C.1–2: inclusions from a tin flux, and about 1% or more of the flux incorporated into crystals, partly into the lattice. Sec. V.B: resistive and susceptibility anomalies at structural and magnetic transitions in films |
