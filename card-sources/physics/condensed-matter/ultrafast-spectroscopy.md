| 2 | doi:10.1007/s11120-009-9454-y | full text | "The principle of ultrafast transient absorption spectroscopy" and Fig. 1: a difference spectrum sums ground-state bleach, stimulated emission and excited-state absorption; coherent artefact and cross-phase modulation at early times are excluded or modelled. "Equipment" section: the fraction excited depends on focus size, concentration and extinction coefficient; lower pulse energies used for annihilation-free conditions, while shorter pulse spacing lets long-lived triplet or charge-separated states build up. "The transient absorption setup": white-light chirp described by a polynomial in the analysis; IRF measured by frequency mixing or transient birefringence; samples flowed or shaken to avoid repeated exposure and degradation. "Collection of transient absorption spectra": spectra before time zero for the baseline; repeated runs to test reproducibility and sample stability. "Data analysis": sequential spectra are generally mixtures; pure-species spectra need a specific kinetic model |

"Practical considerations": solid samples are more susceptible to laser damage and thermal accumulation, requiring control of fluence and repetition rate; positive features may arise from refractive-index changes.
