| 2 | doi:10.1017/S1431927612000244 | full text | Section Extracting EELS Core-loss Edge: because background dominates the variance, PCA of raw EELS can lose interface signals, shift fine-structure peaks by up to an electronvolt and replace an edge by an artefact that tracks a neighbouring edge; background should be removed first. Section Least Squares Estimation: background-extrapolation error passes directly into the signal and grows away from the fit window; low counts make the usual power-law fit biased and unstable; the pre-edge residual should fluctuate about zero. Section Local Background Averaging: averaging creates artefacts at abrupt interfaces and particle edges |

| 4 | doi:10.1098/rsta.2019.0601 | full text | Section Damage of Beam Sensitive Materials by Electrons: knock-on and radiolysis mechanisms; mass loss changes composition measured by EELS or EDX; critical fluence measured from the decay of a diffraction, image or spectral feature, and depends on material and conditions. Abstract: analysis must stay within a dose budget. Section Analytical spectroscopy in the TEM: focused probes raise the fluence; damage changes composition through preferential mass loss and changes bonding; the EELS background rises steeply with thickness; in some oxides fine structure changes at doses where images show no structural change, and a beam-induced change in Fe valence was measured |

Introduction: standards-based k-factor quantification works well only under stated conditions, such as thin sample and standards (t/λ below about 0.3) |
| 6 | doi:10.1186/s42649-026-00143-9 | full text | Abstract and Introduction: unlike core-loss signals, low-loss excitations are strongly delocalised, so the effective spatial resolution can be much broader than the probe size. Section Sources of uncertainty and spectral artifacts: residual energy drift, spectrometer instability and imperfect energy calibration introduce systematic shifts in extracted energies |
| 7 | doi:10.1007/s10853-014-8685-2 | full text | Section The rise of standardless quantitative analysis: one commercial standardless method needed about ±30 % relative error to capture 95 % of results, and internal normalisation to unity loses the analytical total. Section Specimen geometry effects: topography and surface inclination change measured intensities in ways unrelated to composition; matrix corrections assume a flat, polished specimen. Section Qualitative analysis failures: automatic peak identification mislabels a few per cent of major-constituent peaks and more minor and trace ones; confirm by peak fitting, residual inspection and other beam energies. Section The steps to successful quantitative SEM/SDD-EDS: polished, unetched surfaces; coating thickness matters at low beam energy; dead time about 10 % to limit coincidence peaks. Sections on low atomic number elements and Limits of detection: oxygen is often calculated from assumed stoichiometry; coincidence peaks mimic trace elements; detection limits estimated from peak and background counts |
