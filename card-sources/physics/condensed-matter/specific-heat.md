| 4 | doi:10.1007/s10765-022-03046-5 | full text | "Theory" and "'Calorimetric' Debye temperatures and their fit": the Debye model suits only simple isotropic crystals and the calorimetric Debye temperature varies with temperature; a nuclear term matters below about 1 K, depends on field and falls as 1/T² above its peak; a low-temperature linear term need not come from conduction electrons and can come from lattice vacancies. "Determination of Lattice Heat Capacity": the lattice term must be estimated over the whole range of an anomaly, for example by fitting regions it does not affect or by comparison with an isostructural compound. "Heat-pulse relaxation calorimetry (PPMS)": accuracy depends on sample mass and geometry, is lower for powders than crystals, and earlier software failed at first-order transitions. "Differential scanning calorimetry": the three-curve method, accuracy about 1–2%, heat-flux instruments less accurate. "Important notes for experimental cP data": small samples must be pure and homogeneous; dynamic methods make sharp peaks lower, broader and shifted |
