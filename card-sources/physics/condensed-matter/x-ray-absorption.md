| 1 | doi:10.1038/s43586-025-00397-9 | full text | "Experimental setup": electron escape depth below 10 nm, so electron yield is surface sensitive; fluorescence yield more bulk sensitive; inverse partial fluorescence yield as an alternative. "Sample requirements": electron yield sensitive to top layers that differ from the bulk in stoichiometry, valence and anisotropy; capping layers must not alter the magnetism; signs of charging, including the time response and a reduced L3 to L2 ratio; beam damage such as reduction of transition-metal oxides. "Data analysis": transmission needs a homogeneous sample of constant thickness without pinholes, with no reliable correction otherwise; fluorescence reabsorption must be corrected for sum rules, and soft X-ray fluorescence decay prevents quantitative use; average of circular spectra equals the isotropic spectrum only without linear dichroism; step background in a 2:1 ratio, approximate and usually unsuitable for ionic compounds; helicity and field reversal equivalent only without optical activity; sum rules agree with bulk to about 10%. "Reproducibility and data deposition": show both circular spectra with pre-edge at zero and post-edge at one, report temperature, field direction and history, degree of polarisation and incidence angle; sensitivity to strain, defects and impurity phases; assess beam damage. "Limitations and optimizations": hole number not measurable absolutely, ratio of orbital to spin moment independent of it, dipole term up to 50% error at surfaces, obtainable from angle-dependent data, jj-mixing correction factors (1.47 for Mn d5, 2 for Cr d3); electron-yield saturation at shallow angles and field effects, fluorescence self-absorption, degree of polarisation, pairs of spectra with opposite magnetisation, element-specific hysteresis loops |
