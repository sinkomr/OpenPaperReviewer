## 2. Abstract (verbatim)

> We compared tomato yield in raised beds mulched with 10 cm of straw against unmulched beds in a randomized complete block trial with six blocks at one site in one season. Mulched plots yielded 4.10 kg per plant and unmulched plots 3.20 kg per plant, a difference of 0.90 kg per plant (95% confidence interval 0.59 to 1.21; paired t-test, p < 0.001), which is 28% of the unmulched yield. We did not measure soil moisture, weed cover or soil temperature, so the trial does not identify why mulch raised yield.

## 5. Methods summary

**Stated.**
- Site: one site, one season. Twelve raised beds, 1.2 m × 2.4 m, in six blocks of two.
- Allocation: within each block, one bed to mulch and one to no mulch, by coin toss.
- Plants: eight plants per bed, one cultivar, transplanted the same day.
- Treatment: 10 cm of wheat straw applied one week after transplanting.
- Common management: same drip-irrigation schedule and fertilizer in all beds; weeds pulled from every bed weekly.
- Harvest: picked twice a week for ten weeks, weighed per bed. Yield per plant = bed total / 8.
- Unit of replication: the bed; six independent pairs.
- Blinding: none; pickers and weighers could see treatment.
- Statistics: paired t-test across six blocks.
- Exclusions: none of beds or harvests.

**Not stated.**
- Location, soil, year, dates, weather.
- Cultivar name.
- Criteria for blocking (what the blocks were formed along) and the layout of beds within and between blocks.
- Fertilizer type and rate; irrigation amount.
- Ripeness criterion for picking; whether fruit remaining at the end of the ten weeks was counted; whether culled or damaged fruit was weighed.
- Whether any plants died or were replaced (the divisor is fixed at eight).
- Scale type and calibration.
- How the sample size of six blocks was chosen.
- Whether the analysis was planned in advance.
- Whether the straw source was tested or whether straw carried seeds or residues.

- Q-1. Did any plants die, and if so, is per-plant yield still bed total / 8?

## 11. Summary

The paper reports a small field trial at one site in one season. Twelve raised beds of tomatoes were paired into six blocks; in each block one bed, chosen by coin toss, was covered with 10 cm of wheat straw and the other was left bare. Over ten weeks of harvesting, mulched beds yielded 4.10 kg of fruit per plant and bare beds 3.20 kg, a gain of 0.90 kg per plant (about 28%), with mulch ahead in every block. The authors say the trial cannot tell whether the gain came from moisture, weeds or soil temperature, since none was measured, that the result may not hold at other sites, seasons or cultivars, and that the harvesters knew which beds were mulched.
