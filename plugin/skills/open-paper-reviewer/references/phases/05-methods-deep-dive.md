# Phase 5: Methods deep-dive

Purpose: look at the methods as a whole. A paper can have claims that each look defensible and still be wrong, because a calibration, a processing step or a missing control affects all of them at once. The reverse also happens: the methods can be solid in ways a claim-by-claim review never credits.

## Topics

Choose four to ten topics that matter for this paper, from:

- each main measurement technique, as it was actually used;
- preparation and characterization of samples or subjects;
- calibration, controls and nulls;
- data processing: background subtraction, smoothing, fitting, selection or exclusion of data;
- statistics: sample size, replication, uncertainty, and how significance was judged;
- consistency between techniques: do quantities obtained in different ways agree in scale and units?
- availability of data, code and supplementary material.

Processing steps interact, so consider them together and not one at a time.

For each topic use the `methods-topic` template: what the paper does, with locations; what standard practice requires, with sources; the gap, if any, and the claims it affects; and what would close it. Where the paper's practice is sound, say so in the same place.

## Checklist close-out

Go through every item on the Phase 1 checklist and give each an answer: complies, departs, not enough detail to tell, or not applicable, with where in the review it is addressed. Nothing on the list is left unanswered.

## Effect on verdicts

End with the table of topics, claims affected, and any Phase 3 verdict that changes as a result. Make those changes in the Phase 3 files.

Write to `05_methods.md`.
