# Phase 6: Synthesis

Purpose: one document a reader can rely on without opening the others. A competent person in the field who has not read the paper should be able to read it and explain the main findings in their own words. It adds nothing new: every statement in it traces to a claim id, a reference file or a methods topic.

Write `06_synthesis.md` following the `synthesis-outline` template. If your tool tends to cut off long outputs, write it a section at a time and save as you go.

What matters:

- **Header.** Paper identifier and version, with any correction or retraction notice on the paper; review mode, with the user's concerns verbatim if there were any; the model, effort and tool; and the citation-verification line, which Phase 7 sets.
- **What the paper establishes comes before what it does not.** List the claims that stand and say what they amount to, including methods that were done well.
- **Main problems** are ordered by severity. Each names the claims involved, the evidence, and what would resolve it.
- **Concerns.** In a focused review, answer each concern explicitly, including "nothing found".
- **What would settle the open questions.** Draw the experiments and analyses from the claim blocks, most decisive first, and mark the smallest set that would resolve the Critical findings.
- **Headline claims.** Give each an assessment: established, established in part, not established, or contradicted. Give a recommendation in a journal's terms (accept, revise, reject) only if the user asked for one.
- **Plain language.** The summary includes a few sentences a non-specialist can follow.
- **Appendices.** The verdict for every claim, the sources with their verification status, and the limits of this review.

Do not add the verdicts up into a score, and do not characterize the authors. The subject is the paper.

The synthesis is a draft until Phase 7 is complete.
