# Evidence in proportion to the claim

Whether the strength of the evidence matches how much a claim asks the reader to accept.

## 1. Strength of evidence against the size of the claim

**Question.** For each tier-3 claim, is the evidence strong enough to exclude ordinary explanations, or is it the evidence that would be offered for a routine result?

**Why it matters.** The less likely a result is beforehand, the more likely a positive finding of a given quality is to be false. Evidence that is adequate for a routine claim can leave an exceptional one unestablished.

**Resolved by.** Stronger evidence for the exceptional claims than for the routine ones: tighter uncertainties, more samples, and tests aimed at the ordinary explanations.

**Benchmark.** [1]

## 2. Independent lines of evidence

**Question.** Does more than one independent kind of measurement support each headline claim?

**Why it matters.** A single technique cannot separate the claimed effect from an artefact of that technique. Agreement between methods that fail in different ways is much harder to explain away.

**Resolved by.** A second measurement, by a different method, that gives the same answer; or a clear statement that the claim rests on one method.

**Benchmark.** None needed: this is a matter of logic.

## 3. Conclusions matched to results

**Question.** Does each conclusion in the abstract and the final section correspond to a specific result in the body, stated no more strongly than that result?

**Why it matters.** Abstracts and conclusions are what most readers take away. A statement there that outruns the results is a claim made without evidence.

**Resolved by.** Every concluding statement traceable to a figure, table or derivation, with the same scope and the same qualifications.

**Benchmark.** None needed: this is a matter of logic.

## 4. Values beyond established limits

**Question.** Does any quoted or plotted value lie beyond a limit set by established theory, or far beyond the best values reported by others, and if so does the paper say so and account for it?

**Why it matters.** A value outside an established limit is either a discovery or the sign of an error in the measurement, the analysis or the description of the sample. Such values are not always among the claims a paper draws attention to. A paper that reports one without comment has not shown which of the two it is.

**Resolved by.** The limit or the previous best value stated, the difference acknowledged, and evidence aimed at the ordinary sources of error.

**Benchmark.** [1]
