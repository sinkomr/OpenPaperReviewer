# Controls and nulls

Whether the design can tell the claimed effect apart from everything else that could produce the same result.

## 1. Control comparison

**Question.** Is there a comparison in which the claimed effect should be absent, and is it shown to be absent there?

**Why it matters.** Without a control, an effect of the apparatus, the procedure or the sample handling cannot be distinguished from the effect being claimed.

**Resolved by.** A stated control group or control measurement for each main result, or a stated reason why none was used.

**Benchmark.** [1]

## 2. Protection against expectation

**Question.** Were steps taken to stop expectations shaping the data: randomized allocation or ordering, blinding of whoever collected or analysed the data, or an analysis fixed before the data were seen?

**Why it matters.** People who know which result is hoped for tend to find it, in how they collect, select and analyse data, without intending to.

**Resolved by.** A statement of whether randomization and blinding were used, and how; or a statement that they were not.

**Benchmark.** [1], [2]

## 3. Alternatives tested

**Question.** For each headline interpretation, is every ordinary alternative tested by a measurement, and not only argued against in the text?

**Why it matters.** An argument that an alternative is unlikely is weaker than a measurement that excludes it, and is easy to make for the explanation the authors prefer.

**Resolved by.** For each alternative, a specific measurement or analysis whose outcome differs between it and the claimed interpretation.

**Benchmark.** None needed: this is a matter of logic.
