# Internal consistency

Whether the paper agrees with itself.

## 1. The same quantity, the same value

**Question.** Does each quantity have the same value, with the same units, wherever it appears: abstract, text, tables, figures, captions and supplementary material?

**Why it matters.** A value that changes between sections leaves the reader unable to say which one the conclusions rest on.

**Resolved by.** One value per quantity throughout, or an explanation where two legitimately differ.

**Benchmark.** None needed: this is a matter of logic.

## 2. Numbers that can be recomputed

**Question.** Can the statistics and derived numbers the paper quotes be reproduced from the values it gives?

**Why it matters.** Reported statistics that do not follow from their own inputs are common in published work, and some of them change the conclusion.

**Resolved by.** Recomputing percentages, ratios, test statistics and unit conversions from the reported inputs and getting the reported outputs.

**Benchmark.** [1]

## 3. Figures that show what the text says

**Question.** Does each figure show what the text says it shows, with axes, units, scales and labels that allow the reader to check?

**Why it matters.** Claims are often carried by a figure. If the figure cannot be read quantitatively, or shows something other than the text describes, the claim has no visible support.

**Resolved by.** Labelled axes with units, scale bars on images, legends that identify every element, and text that matches the figure.

**Benchmark.** None needed: this is a matter of logic.

## 4. Separate measurements, separate data

**Question.** Where two figures or panels are presented as separate measurements (different samples, conditions or times), do they show different data?

**Why it matters.** Independent measurements do not share their noise or their fine detail, even on the same sample. A trace or image that appears twice under different labels, possibly rescaled, shifted or cropped, is one measurement, so at least one of the labels describes something the figure does not show.

**Resolved by.** Panels whose noise and fine detail differ as independent measurements do, or a statement that a panel is repeated, rescaled or replotted from another.

**Benchmark.** [2]
