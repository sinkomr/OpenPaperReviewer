# Use of citations

Whether the works a paper cites carry the weight the paper puts on them.

## 1. The source says what it is cited for

**Question.** For each reference a claim leans on, does the cited work actually report what the paper attributes to it?

**Why it matters.** A substantial share of citations in published articles do not support the statement they are attached to. A claim that rests on such a citation rests on nothing.

**Resolved by.** Reading the cited work and finding the attributed result in it.

**Benchmark.** [1]

## 2. Used within its conditions

**Question.** Is each cited result applied within the conditions of the original work: the same system or population, the same regime, the same quantity?

**Why it matters.** A result established for one material, scale, temperature or population does not automatically hold for another. A theoretical prediction is not an observation.

**Resolved by.** The conditions of the cited work stated where they differ from the paper's, with a reason why the result still applies.

**Benchmark.** None needed: this is a matter of logic.

## 3. Complete and resolvable

**Question.** Does every citation resolve to a real, correctly described work, with no placeholders or missing entries?

**Why it matters.** A reference that cannot be found cannot be checked, and a placeholder marks support that was intended and never supplied.

**Resolved by.** A reference list in which every entry can be located from the details given.

**Benchmark.** None needed: this can be checked directly.

## 4. Contrary work acknowledged

**Question.** Does the paper cite directly relevant earlier work, including work that reports the opposite or a null result?

**Why it matters.** Leaving out a known alternative explanation or a contrary result makes a claim look better supported than it is.

**Resolved by.** The relevant earlier results cited and addressed. A reviewer raising an omission names the specific work.

**Benchmark.** None needed: this is a matter of logic.
